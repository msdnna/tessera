A reminder is a one-off note about a time: some text plus the moment when you need to be reminded of it. The section opens from the **Reminders** item in the side menu (the “☰” button at the left of the top bar).

**The app is the delivery.** The web version shows a reminder in the list but sends no system notification — the signal at the right minute comes from right here. If you set up a reminder on your computer while the phone with the app is nearby, it will fire on the phone.

![The Reminders section in the app](../assets/reminders-mobile-light.png)

## Creating and closing

In the row at the top: the text in the “What should we remind you about?” field, the time in the date picker, then **Add**. Without the text or the time a reminder won't be created — the app will ask you to fill in both fields.

A reminder that has fired is marked with a checkmark on the left: it stays in the list as done, and you can clear the mark with the same tap. An unwanted one is removed with the trash icon on the right.

## Getting the signal on time

The notification is set as an alarm on the device itself, so whether it rings depends on the system permissions:

- **Notification permission.** Android 13 and newer asks for it separately; deny it and reminders will pile up in the list silently. It's turned on in the app's system settings, under “Notifications”.
- **Battery saving.** If the system has put the app to sleep, the alarm may fire later than scheduled. On phones with aggressive battery saving it's worth allowing Tessera to run in the background.

The app asks for the notification permission on first launch; denying it doesn't break anything else — only the signals stop coming, and the permission can always be granted later in the system settings. A reminder survives a phone reboot: the alarms are set again at the next start.

## How it differs from a task's due date

A **due date** lives on a task: it shows on the card, the board is filtered and sorted by it, and the calendar, timeline and Gantt are built from it. A due date answers the question “when does this need to be done”.

A **reminder** is a standalone record, tied to nothing, and it answers the question “when should I be reminded of this”. A call, an email, a promise to ring back — those are all reminders, not tasks with a due date.
